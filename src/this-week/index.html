Caramelized Custard Tart Cups

Jump to Behind the Scenes

Why Muffin Pans?

These Portuguese-inspired custard tart cups capture the blistered, caramelized tops and shattering layers of traditional pastéis de nata, reimagined for a standard muffin pan. A quick laminated puff pastry base hugs a silky lemon-cinnamon custard that bakes into golden, freckled rounds that lift cleanly from the tin.

Prep 15 mins
Cook 20 mins
Yield 12 servings
Difficulty Medium

Ingredients

  • 1 sheet (8-9 oz) Frozen puff pastry sheet, thawed
  • 1 cup Granulated sugar
  • 1/3 cup Water
  • 1 stick (about 3 inches) Cinnamon stick
  • 1 strip (about 3 inches) Lemon peel strip (Yellow part only, avoid white pith if possible)
  • 1 cup Whole milk
  • 3 tbsp All-purpose flour
  • 1/8 tsp Kosher salt
  • 4 Large egg yolks
  • 1 tsp Pure vanilla extract
  • 1/8 tsp Ground cinnamon (Optional, for dusting after baking)
  • 1 tbsp Nonstick cooking spray or unsalted butter (Softened, for greasing muffin pan)

Instructions

  1. 01Preheat the oven to 500°F with a rack in the upper third. Lightly grease a standard 12-cup muffin pan with nonstick cooking spray or softened unsalted butter, making sure to coat the bottoms and sides of each well.
  2. 02Make the sugar syrup: In a small saucepan, combine the granulated sugar, water, cinnamon stick, and lemon peel strip. Set over medium heat and cook, swirling occasionally, until the sugar dissolves and the mixture comes to a gentle boil. Continue boiling without stirring for 2-3 minutes, just until slightly thickened but still clear. Remove from the heat and let the syrup cool while you prepare the custard base. Leave the cinnamon stick and lemon peel in the syrup to infuse.
  3. 03Make the milk-flour base: In a separate medium saucepan off the heat, whisk together the all-purpose flour and kosher salt. Gradually whisk in the whole milk, a little at a time, until completely smooth with no lumps.
  4. 04Cook the milk mixture: Place the saucepan with the milk mixture over medium heat. Cook, whisking constantly, until it thickens to a smooth, pudding-like consistency that lightly coats the back of a spoon, 3-5 minutes. Remove from the heat and let cool for 5 minutes, whisking occasionally to keep it smooth.
  5. 05Prepare the egg mixture: In a medium bowl, whisk the large egg yolks until smooth. Slowly drizzle the slightly cooled milk mixture into the yolks, whisking constantly, to temper them and prevent scrambling.
  6. 06Finish the custard: Strain the cooled sugar syrup through a fine-mesh sieve directly into the egg-milk mixture, discarding the cinnamon stick and lemon peel. Whisk until fully combined, then whisk in the pure vanilla extract. The custard will be fairly thin and pourable. Set aside while you shape the pastry cups.
  7. 07Shape the puff pastry: Unfold the thawed puff pastry sheet on a lightly floured surface, smoothing out any creases. Starting from one short end, roll the pastry up tightly into a log, creating a spiral. Using a sharp knife, cut the log crosswise into 12 equal rounds.
  8. 08Form the tart cups: Working with one round at a time, place a pastry round cut-side up in a muffin cup. With your thumbs, press and stretch the dough outward and up the sides of the well, keeping the spiral pattern on the bottom and forming an even shell that reaches just to the rim. Repeat with the remaining pastry rounds to line all 12 muffin cups.
  9. 09Fill with custard: Give the custard a quick whisk, then pour it into each pastry-lined muffin cup, filling each about 3/4 full. Divide the custard evenly among all 12 cups; you should use all of it.
  10. 10Bake the tart cups: Place the muffin pan on the upper oven rack. Bake at 500°F for 15-20 minutes, rotating the pan halfway through, until the pastry is deeply golden, the custard is puffed with dark caramelized spots on top, and the centers still wobble slightly when the pan is gently shaken.
  11. 11Optional broil for extra blistering: If the tops are not as freckled as you like but the pastry edges look done, switch the oven to broil and broil the tart cups for 1-2 minutes, watching closely, until the custard tops develop additional dark spots. Do not walk away; they can go from perfect to burnt quickly.
  12. 12Cool and finish: Transfer the muffin pan to a wire rack and let the tart cups cool in the pan for 10-15 minutes; the custard will set as they cool. Run a thin knife around the edges if needed, then gently lift each tart cup out of the pan. Dust lightly with the optional ground cinnamon just before serving, if desired. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef's Notes

The tight spiral of rolled puff pastry mimics traditional Portuguese dough, giving you those flaky, layered sides that really show off the muffin-cup shape. Press the dough up the sides firmly so it doesn't slump down as the fat melts. High heat is non-negotiable for proper blistering; if your oven doesn't reach 500°F reliably, preheat a sturdy baking sheet and set the muffin tin on top to boost bottom heat. Watch through the door instead of opening it repeatedly so the temperature stays steady. The custard should still wobble slightly in the center when you pull them; overbaking leads to a curdled, grainy interior. If the tops aren't spotting but the edges look done, switch briefly to broil for 1-2 minutes, watching like a hawk. Store leftovers in the fridge and re-crisp in a 400°F oven for 5-7 minutes. The texture won't be quite as ethereal as fresh, but the muffin-shaped cups will hold together beautifully and the pastry will regain some of its shatter.
