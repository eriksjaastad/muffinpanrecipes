Miso Ginger Donburi Cups

Jump to Behind the Scenes

Why Muffin Pans?

Tender miso-ginger glazed salmon is tucked into crisp nori-sesame rice bases that bake into sturdy, round cups you can lift straight from the pan. Each molded cup eats like a single-serving salmon donburi, finished with scallions, cucumber, and a touch of Japanese mayo for richness.

Prep 15 mins
Cook 20 mins
Yield 12 servings
Difficulty Medium

Ingredients

  • 1 cup short-grain sushi rice, uncooked (rinsed until water runs mostly clear)
  • 1 1/4 cups water
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt (for rice)
  • 1 tbsp unseasoned rice vinegar
  • 1 tsp granulated sugar
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • 2 full sheets nori (roasted seaweed, finely shredded)
  • 3 tsp toasted sesame seeds (2 tsp for rice, 1 tsp for garnish)
  • 1 lb skinless salmon fillet (center-cut, pin bones removed)
  • 2 tbsp white miso paste (shiro miso)
  • 1 tbsp plus 1 tsp low-sodium soy sauce (divided)
  • 1 tbsp mirin
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger (very finely grated)
  • 1 small garlic clove (very finely grated)
  • 1 tsp plus more neutral oil (canola or grapeseed, plus more for greasing pan)
  • 2 tbsp Kewpie mayonnaise (Japanese)
  • 1 tsp fresh lime juice (or rice vinegar)
  • 2 scallions (thinly sliced, greens and whites separated)
  • 1 small Persian cucumber (very thinly sliced)

Instructions

  1. 01Cook the rice: In a small saucepan, combine the rinsed sushi rice, 1 1/4 cups water, and 1/2 tsp kosher salt. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat, stir once, cover, reduce heat to low, and cook for 15 minutes. Turn off the heat and let stand, covered, for 10 minutes.
  2. 02Shred the nori: While the rice cooks, stack the 2 full nori sheets and use kitchen scissors to finely shred them into thin strips or confetti-sized pieces. Set aside.
  3. 03Season the rice: In a small bowl, stir together the unseasoned rice vinegar, granulated sugar, and toasted sesame oil until the sugar dissolves. Fluff the hot rice into a wide bowl, drizzle with the vinegar mixture, add the shredded nori and 2 tsp of the toasted sesame seeds, and gently fold until evenly combined. Let cool until just warm, about 10 minutes.
  4. 04Prep the pan and oven: Preheat the oven to 400°F. Lightly grease a standard 12-cup muffin tin with a little neutral oil or nonstick spray, making sure to coat the sides and bottoms of each well.
  5. 05Form the rice bases: Divide the seasoned rice evenly among the 12 wells (about 2 tbsp per cup). With damp fingers, press the rice firmly into the bottom and slightly up the sides of each well, forming a compact, shallow rice cup. Pack it tightly so it will hold together after baking. Set aside.
  6. 06Mix the miso glaze: In a medium bowl, whisk together the white miso paste, 1 tbsp of the low-sodium soy sauce, mirin, honey, grated fresh ginger, and grated garlic until smooth.
  7. 07Prepare the salmon: Pat the salmon dry with paper towels and cut into 12 equal cubes, about 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 inches each (trim thinner edges as needed so pieces are fairly uniform). Add the salmon cubes to the miso mixture and gently toss to coat all sides. Let sit for 10 minutes while the oven finishes heating.
  8. 08Assemble cups: Place one glazed salmon cube, glaze side up, on top of the rice in each well, pressing it down slightly so it nests into the rice but still sits proudly in the center. Spoon any remaining miso glaze evenly over the salmon pieces. Drizzle the 1 tsp neutral oil over the tops to encourage browning.
  9. 09Bake: Place the muffin tin on the middle rack and bake at 400°F for 14-16 minutes, until the salmon is just opaque in the center and flakes easily with a fork and the rice edges look slightly golden and set.
  10. 10Make the drizzle: While the cups bake, stir together the Kewpie mayonnaise, the remaining 1 tsp low-sodium soy sauce, and fresh lime juice in a small bowl. Thin with a few drops of water if needed so it's drizzleable.
  11. 11Cool and release: Let the cups rest in the pan for 5 minutes to firm. Run a thin knife around each cup to loosen the rice from the sides, then gently lift each portion out with a small offset spatula or fork, keeping the round, molded rice base intact under the salmon.
  12. 12Finish and serve: Transfer the cups to a platter. Drizzle lightly with the soy-lime mayo, sprinkle with the remaining 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds, and top with scallion greens and a few Persian cucumber slices. Scatter the sliced scallion whites over the platter for extra bite and color. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef's Notes

The rice must be packed firmly into the wells; if it's too loose, the cups won't hold together. Slightly warm, just-seasoned rice is easiest to mold and binds well as it bakes. Center-cut salmon gives you uniform cubes that cook evenly; if your fillet tapers, use the thicker section and save thin ends for another use. Don't overbake - pull them as soon as the salmon is just cooked to keep it silky. These are excellent at room temperature, so they travel well for lunches or picnics. For a fuller meal, serve two or three cups per person with a simple side of blanched snap peas dressed in soy and sesame oil.
