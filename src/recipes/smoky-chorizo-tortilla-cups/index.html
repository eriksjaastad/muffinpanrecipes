Smoky Chorizo Tortilla Cups

Jump to Behind the Scenes

Why Muffin Pans?

Corn tortillas are pressed into the muffin wells to form crisp, golden cups, then filled with smoky Spanish chorizo, sautéed peppers and onions, and a rich paprika‑spiked egg custard. Each round, flared cup bakes up into a self-contained bite with a tender center and crisp edges, perfect with a dollop of garlicky tomato aioli. These travel well, reheat beautifully, and hit that sweet spot between tapas and brunch.

Prep 25 mins
Cook 22 mins
Yield 12 servings
Difficulty Medium

Ingredients

  • 12 small corn tortillas (5 to 6 inch, flexible, not super thick)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil (plus more for brushing)
  • 8 oz cured Spanish chorizo (not fresh Mexican; casings removed, finely diced)
  • 1/2 cup finely diced yellow onion
  • 1/2 cup finely diced red bell pepper
  • 1/4 cup finely diced roasted red pepper (jarred, drained well)
  • 2 cloves garlic (minced)
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika (Spanish pimentón dulce)
  • 1/4 tsp hot smoked paprika (or a pinch of cayenne, optional)
  • 1/4 tsp kosher salt (plus a pinch for veggies)
  • 1/8 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 6 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup whole milk
  • 1/4 cup heavy cream
  • 1/2 cup finely grated Manchego cheese (about 2 oz, packed lightly)
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh flat-leaf parsley (plus more for garnish)
  • Nonstick cooking spray
  • 1/3 cup mayonnaise
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped oil-packed sun-dried tomatoes (patted dry)
  • 1 small garlic clove, very finely minced or grated
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • Pinch kosher salt

Instructions

  1. 01Preheat the oven to 375°F. Place a rack in the center. Lightly spray a standard 12-cup muffin tin with nonstick spray, then brush each well with a thin film of olive oil for extra crispness.
  2. 02Soften the tortillas so they mold without cracking: Wrap the stack of tortillas in a damp paper towel, place on a microwave-safe plate, and microwave for 30-40 seconds until warm and pliable. Keep them wrapped so they stay soft as you work.
  3. 03Form the tortilla cups: Working one at a time, lightly brush both sides of each warm tortilla with olive oil. Gently press it down into a muffin well, pleating the sides to fit snugly so the tortilla lines the bottom and comes all the way up the sides. Press firmly against the curves of the cup so it takes on the round, flared shape. Repeat for all 12 wells.
  4. 04Par-bake the tortilla shells: Bake the empty tortilla cups for 7-8 minutes, until the edges are just starting to turn light golden and the tortillas hold their molded shape. Remove the pan and set aside while you prepare the filling. Keep the oven at 375°F.
  5. 05Make the chorizo filling: In a medium skillet, heat 1 tbsp olive oil over medium heat. Add the diced Spanish chorizo and cook 3-4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until some of the fat renders and the edges begin to crisp.
  6. 06Add the fresh onion and red bell pepper to the skillet with a pinch of salt. Cook, stirring, 4-5 minutes until softened. Stir in the roasted red pepper and garlic; cook 1 minute more until fragrant. Sprinkle in the smoked paprika, hot smoked paprika (if using), black pepper, and another small pinch of salt. Stir to coat, then remove from heat and let the mixture cool for 3-4 minutes so it doesn't scramble the eggs later.
  7. 07Prepare the egg custard: In a medium bowl, whisk together the eggs, milk, heavy cream, 1/4 tsp kosher salt, and a few grinds of black pepper until well combined but not overly frothy. Stir in the grated Manchego and chopped parsley.
  8. 08Fill the cups: Divide the slightly cooled chorizo mixture evenly among the 12 tortilla cups (about 2 tbsp per cup), spreading it in an even layer over the bottom so it hugs the curved base. Give the egg mixture a quick stir, then pour it over the chorizo in each cup, filling to just below the top of the tortilla edge. The filling should come up about 3/4 of the way; don't overfill or they'll mushroom over the sides.
  9. 09Bake: Return the pan to the oven and bake at 375°F for 14-16 minutes, until the egg filling is just set in the center and slightly puffed, and the tortilla edges are deep golden and crisp. A toothpick inserted in the center of one cup should come out mostly clean with just a hint of moisture, not wet egg.
  10. 10Rest and release: Transfer the muffin tin to a wire rack and let the cups rest in the pan for 5-7 minutes. Run a thin knife around the edge of each cup, angling the blade toward the metal to avoid cutting the tortilla. Gently lift each chorizo tortilla cup straight up from the well. They should hold together as round, molded cups with crisp, flared sides.
  11. 11Make the tomato aioli (optional): While the cups bake or rest, stir together the mayonnaise, sun-dried tomatoes, garlic, lemon juice, olive oil, and a pinch of salt in a small bowl. Taste and adjust salt or lemon as needed.
  12. 12Serve: Arrange the warm cups on a platter, spoon or pipe a small dollop of tomato aioli on top of each, and sprinkle with a little extra chopped parsley. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef's Notes

- Spanish vs. Mexican chorizo: You need cured Spanish chorizo here - firm, sliceable, and already cooked - so it dices neatly and doesn't release too much grease. Fresh Mexican chorizo will be too loose and oily for these tidy cups. - Tortilla texture: Corn tortillas vary. If yours are very stiff or crack when bent, warm them slightly longer and don't skip brushing with oil; the oil helps them mold cleanly to the round wells and crisp evenly. - Make-ahead: These keep well. Cool completely, refrigerate in an airtight container up to 3 days. Reheat on a sheet pan at 325°F for 8-10 minutes; the tortilla "shell" will re-crisp and the egg will warm through without drying. - Variations: Swap Manchego for a sharp aged cheddar if you can't find it, but keep the smoked paprika - that's where the Spanish character lives. A few finely chopped olives or a spoonful of sautéed spinach can be folded into the filling without affecting structure.
