Salsa Verde Chilaquiles Cups

Jump to Behind the Scenes

Why Muffin Pans?

Corn tortilla strips are baked into crisp, saucy cups with roasted tomatillo salsa, black beans, and melty Oaxaca cheese, then finished with crema, avocado, and cilantro. These chilaquiles cups hold their shape straight from the muffin pan but eat like a cozy Mexican brunch, with bright heat and satisfying crunch in every bite.

Prep 30 mins
Cook 25 mins
Yield 12 servings
Difficulty Medium

Ingredients

  • 12 small corn tortillas (6-inch, preferably white corn)
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil (plus more for brushing, if needed)
  • 1 1/4 tsp kosher salt (divided)
  • 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1/2 tsp chili powder (mild ancho if possible)
  • 1 1/2 cups salsa verde (preferably roasted tomatillo style, not too thin)
  • 1 cup canned black beans (drained and rinsed)
  • 1 1/4 cups shredded Oaxaca cheese (or low-moisture mozzarella as substitute)
  • 1/2 cup crumbled queso fresco
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tbsp sour cream or Mexican crema
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh cilantro (plus more for garnish)
  • 1/4 cup finely diced red onion
  • 1 small jalapeño, seeded and finely minced (optional, for heat)
  • 1 ripe avocado, diced (for topping)
  • 1/4 cup Mexican crema or sour cream (for drizzling)
  • Lime wedges (for serving)
  • Nonstick cooking spray

Instructions

  1. 01Preheat the oven to 375°F. Lightly grease a standard 12-cup muffin tin with nonstick spray, making sure to coat the sides and rims of each well.
  2. 02Stack the corn tortillas and cut them into 1/2-inch wide strips, then cut the strips in half crosswise so you have short, thin pieces. You should have about 6 cups of loosely piled tortilla strips.
  3. 03In a large bowl, whisk together 3 tbsp vegetable oil, 1 tsp kosher salt, ground cumin, and chili powder. Add the tortilla strips and toss thoroughly with your hands, separating the strips so they all get a light coating of seasoned oil.
  4. 04Divide the seasoned tortilla strips among the 12 muffin wells, about 1/2 cup loosely packed per cup. Use your fingers to press the strips firmly into the bottom and up the sides of each well, overlapping and layering them so there are no big gaps; this is your "shell." The cups will look very full and shaggy - that's good.
  5. 05Bake the tortilla shells at 375°F for 10-12 minutes, until the exposed edges are starting to crisp and lightly brown but not fully hard. Remove the pan and leave the oven on. The shells will finish crisping later.
  6. 06While the shells bake, in a medium bowl whisk together salsa verde, remaining 1/4 tsp kosher salt, eggs, and 2 tbsp sour cream or Mexican crema until smooth. Stir in the black beans, shredded Oaxaca cheese, 2 tbsp chopped cilantro, red onion, and jalapeño (if using). The mixture should be thick but spoonable.
  7. 07Using a measuring cup or ladle, spoon the salsa verde filling into the par-baked tortilla shells, about 3 tbsp per cup. The mixture should sit just below the top of the tortilla edges; gently nudge any stray tortilla strips back into cup shape so they bake into a cohesive rim.
  8. 08Return the muffin tin to the oven and bake at 375°F for 13-15 minutes, until the filling is set in the center (it should not jiggle when you gently shake the pan) and the tortilla edges are deep golden and crisp. If any cups look pale at the edges, give them an extra 2-3 minutes.
  9. 09Remove the pan from the oven and let the chilaquiles cups rest in the tin for 8-10 minutes. This rest is crucial so the tortilla shells firm up and the filling tightens enough to hold together.
  10. 10Run a thin knife around the outer edge of each cup to release any stuck cheese or tortilla. Carefully lift each chilaquiles cup out by its crisp rim or slide a small offset spatula underneath to support the bottom. Transfer to a serving platter or plates.
  11. 11Top each warm cup with a small spoonful of crumbled queso fresco, a few cubes of avocado, a drizzle of Mexican crema or sour cream, and a pinch of fresh cilantro. Serve with lime wedges for squeezing over the top.

Chef's Notes

- The salsa verde should be moderately thick; if yours is very runny, simmer it on the stove for 5-7 minutes to reduce before using, or the cups may turn soggy instead of crisp. - Press the tortilla strips firmly into the wells, especially at the base, so the bottom bakes into a solid disk - this is what lets each portion come out as a self-contained cup instead of falling apart. - These reheat well: cool completely, refrigerate in an airtight container, then warm in a 350°F oven for 8-10 minutes to re-crisp. For a heartier brunch, top each hot cup with a tiny fried quail egg or a spoonful of scrambled eggs right before serving.
