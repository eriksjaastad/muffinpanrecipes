Lemon Ricotta Polenta Cups

Jump to Behind the Scenes

Why Muffin Pans?

These tender Italian-inspired cups layer a crisp, buttery polenta base with a creamy lemon-ricotta custard that bakes up just set and lightly golden. Each round, molded portion pops out of the tin as a self-contained, cheesecake-adjacent bite with a pleasantly sandy cornmeal crust and bright citrus finish. Serve warm or at room temperature with a dusting of powdered sugar and a few fresh berries.

Prep 25 mins
Cook 30 mins
Yield 12 servings
Difficulty Medium

Ingredients

  • 1 cup water
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 cup finely ground yellow cornmeal (polenta, not coarse grits)
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 1/4 cup grated Parmigiano-Reggiano (finely grated)
  • 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp unsalted butter (softened)
  • 2 tbsp fine dry breadcrumbs (plain, not panko)
  • 1 cup whole milk ricotta (drained if very wet)
  • 4 oz cream cheese, softened (half of an 8 oz block)
  • 1/3 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tbsp finely grated lemon zest (from about 2 lemons)
  • 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1/8 tsp kosher salt
  • 2 tbsp powdered sugar (for dusting)
  • 1 cup fresh berries (such as blueberries or sliced strawberries, optional)

Instructions

  1. 01Preheat the oven to 375°F. Place a rack in the middle position. Generously grease all 12 wells of a standard muffin tin with the softened butter, making sure to coat the sides up to the rim. Sprinkle the breadcrumbs evenly into the wells, then tap and rotate the pan so the crumbs lightly coat the bottoms and sides; tap out any excess.
  2. 02Make the polenta base: In a medium saucepan, bring the water, milk, and 1/2 tsp salt just to a simmer over medium heat. While whisking constantly, slowly pour in the cornmeal in a thin stream to avoid lumps. Reduce heat to low and cook, whisking frequently, until very thick and pulling away from the sides, 5-7 minutes.
  3. 03Remove the polenta from the heat and stir in the 2 tbsp butter, grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, and black pepper until smooth. Let it cool for 3-4 minutes, stirring once or twice; it should be thick but still spoonable.
  4. 04Form the polenta cups: Working while the polenta is still warm and pliable, divide it evenly among the 12 muffin wells (about 1 heaping tbsp per cup). Using the back of a spoon or damp fingers, press the polenta firmly into the bottom and about halfway up the sides of each well to form a uniform, round shell. Make sure there are no gaps or thin spots; this base is what will hold the cups together. Set the pan aside.
  5. 05Prepare the lemon ricotta filling: In a medium bowl, beat the ricotta and softened cream cheese together with a hand mixer (or vigorously with a whisk) until smooth, 1-2 minutes, scraping down the sides of the bowl. Add the granulated sugar and beat until combined.
  6. 06Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition. Mix in the lemon zest, lemon juice, vanilla, and 1/8 tsp salt until the mixture is smooth and slightly thickened. Avoid overbeating once the eggs are in; you don't want too much air.
  7. 07Fill the cups: Divide the lemon ricotta mixture evenly among the polenta shells, filling almost to the top of each cup; the filling will puff slightly but should not overflow. Smooth the tops gently with the back of a spoon.
  8. 08Bake: Place the muffin tin on the middle rack and bake at 375°F for 20-24 minutes, until the edges of the polenta are lightly golden and the centers of the filling are just set but still have a slight wobble when you gently jiggle the pan. If the tops start to brown too quickly, rotate the pan or tent loosely with foil for the last few minutes.
  9. 09Cool and release: Transfer the pan to a wire rack and let the cups cool in the tin for 15-20 minutes; this rest helps them firm up and release cleanly. Run a thin knife around the inside edge of each well to loosen, then gently lift each cup out by the side, using the knife or a small offset spatula as support.
  10. 10Serve: Dust the tops lightly with powdered sugar and garnish with fresh berries, if using. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef's Notes

- The polenta must be quite thick; if it still flows easily after 7 minutes, keep cooking and whisking until it holds a mound on the spoon. Thin polenta won't form sturdy shells. - If your ricotta seems very wet, spread it on a paper towel-lined plate and refrigerate 20-30 minutes before using; excess moisture can make the filling loose. - These cups keep well: cool completely, refrigerate in an airtight container up to 3 days. Rewarm in a 300°F oven for 8-10 minutes. They're excellent with espresso or as a not-too-sweet dessert after a rich Italian meal.
