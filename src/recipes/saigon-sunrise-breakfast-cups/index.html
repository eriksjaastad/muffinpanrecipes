Saigon Sunrise Breakfast Cups

Jump to Behind the Scenes

Why Muffin Pans?

These Vietnamese-inspired breakfast cups bake up into tender, custardy rounds packed with caramelized pork, quick-pickled vegetables, and fresh herbs. Each cup carries the flavors of a classic bánh mì - savory, sweet, tangy, and herbaceous - held together in a neat, hand-friendly bite.

Prep 15 mins
Cook 20 mins
Yield 12 servings
Difficulty Medium

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup matchstick carrots
  • 1/2 cup matchstick daikon radish (or mild radish if daikon unavailable)
  • 1/3 cup rice vinegar (unseasoned)
  • 2 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 3/4 tsp kosher salt (divided)
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (canola or vegetable)
  • 8 oz ground pork
  • 2 cloves garlic (minced)
  • 1 green onion (finely sliced, white and green parts separated)
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce (such as Red Boat)
  • 1 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp light brown sugar (packed)
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper (divided)
  • 8 large eggs
  • 1/3 cup whole milk
  • 1/4 cup mayonnaise
  • 1 tsp sriracha (plus more for serving, optional)
  • 1/2 cup finely diced seeded cucumber
  • 1/3 cup fresh cilantro leaves and tender stems (chopped)
  • 1 small jalapeño (thinly sliced, optional, for topping)
  • nonstick cooking spray

Instructions

  1. 01Preheat the oven to 350°F. Place a rack in the center of the oven. Lightly spray a standard 12-cup muffin tin with nonstick cooking spray, making sure to coat the sides and bottoms of each round well.
  2. 02Make the quick pickles: In a small bowl, combine the rice vinegar, granulated sugar, and 1/2 tsp of the kosher salt. Stir until the sugar dissolves. Add the matchstick carrots and daikon, pressing them down so they're mostly submerged. Let sit at room temperature for at least 15 minutes, stirring once or twice. Drain well before using and gently squeeze out excess liquid with your hands or a paper towel so they don't water down the egg mixture.
  3. 03Cook the pork: Heat the neutral oil in a medium skillet over medium heat. Add the ground pork and cook, breaking it into small crumbles, until it loses its pink color, about 4-5 minutes. Add the minced garlic and the white parts of the green onion; cook 1 minute more until fragrant.
  4. 04Season the pork: Stir in the fish sauce, soy sauce, light brown sugar, and 1/4 tsp of the freshly ground black pepper. Cook, stirring frequently, until the liquid is mostly absorbed and the pork looks glossy and slightly caramelized, 2-3 minutes. Remove from heat and let cool for 5-10 minutes so it doesn't scramble the eggs later.
  5. 05Prepare the egg base: In a large bowl, whisk the eggs until well blended and no streaks remain. Add the whole milk, mayonnaise, sriracha, remaining 1/4 tsp kosher salt, and remaining 1/4 tsp black pepper. Whisk until the mixture is smooth and lightly thickened from the mayo - this helps create a custardy texture that holds its round, molded shape.
  6. 06Assemble in the pan: Divide the cooled pork evenly among the 12 muffin cups, about 1 heaping tablespoon per well, pressing it lightly so it forms a loose base that follows the round bottom of each cup. Top each portion of pork with a small pinch of the drained carrot-daikon mixture (about 1 tablespoon per cup), then sprinkle on a few pieces of diced cucumber and a pinch of chopped cilantro (reserve some cilantro for garnish).
  7. 07Pour the eggs: Give the egg mixture a quick whisk. Carefully pour it into each muffin cup, filling each well nearly to the top, about 3/4 full but not overflowing. The eggs will seep down around the pork and vegetables, binding everything into a cohesive round cup as it bakes. If any toppings float, gently nudge them down with a spoon so they're mostly submerged.
  8. 08Bake: Place the muffin tin on the center rack. Bake at 350°F for 18-22 minutes, until the cups are just set in the centers and puffed, and the edges look slightly pulled away from the sides of the wells. A toothpick inserted in the center of one should come out mostly clean with just a bit of moisture, not wet egg. Do not overbake or they'll turn rubbery.
  9. 09Cool and release: Remove the pan from the oven and let the cups cool in the tin for 5-7 minutes; they'll deflate slightly and firm up, taking on that neat, flared muffin-cup shape. Run a thin knife or offset spatula around the edges of each well, then gently lift each round breakfast cup out and transfer to a plate or cooling rack.
  10. 10Garnish and serve: Top each cup with a few slices of jalapeño (if using), a sprinkle of the reserved cilantro, and the sliced green parts of the green onion. Drizzle lightly with extra sriracha or serve with small dipping dishes of sriracha and mayonnaise on the side. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef's Notes

The mayo in the egg mixture is non-negotiable if you want that tender, custardy interior that still unmolds cleanly as a self-contained round - don't skip it. Drain and squeeze the pickled vegetables well; excess liquid will cause the cups to weep and stick. I measure the pork into the wells with a tablespoon so each cup has enough base to anchor the shape. These keep well: cool completely, then refrigerate in an airtight container up to 4 days. Reheat on a sheet pan at 300°F for 8-10 minutes, or eat cold - like leftover bánh mì, the flavors deepen overnight.
