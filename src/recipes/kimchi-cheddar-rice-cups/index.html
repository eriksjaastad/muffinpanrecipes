Kimchi Cheddar Rice Cups

Jump to Behind the Scenes

Why Muffin Pans?

These Korean-inspired rice cups bake into crisp-edged, cheesy rounds packed with tangy kimchi, scallions, and sesame. Each cup holds together like a little rice cake with molten cheddar inside and a spicy gochujang drizzle on top. They're equally good warm from the pan or at room temperature for lunches and parties.

Prep 25 mins
Cook 25 mins
Yield 12 servings
Difficulty Medium

Ingredients

  • 2 cups cooked short-grain white rice (cooled; sushi rice or Calrose style)
  • 1 cup finely chopped napa kimchi (well-drained, packed)
  • 1/2 cup shredded sharp cheddar cheese (plus 1/2 cup for topping)
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tbsp kimchi juice (from the kimchi jar)
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (or tamari)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt (or to taste, depending on kimchi saltiness)
  • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
  • 3 tbsp finely sliced scallions (green parts, plus more for garnish)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (plus more for garnish)
  • 2 tsp neutral oil (for greasing pan; canola or vegetable)
  • 2 tbsp gochujang (Korean red chili paste)
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tbsp water (plus more as needed to thin)
  • 1 tsp soy sauce

Instructions

  1. 01Preheat the oven to 400°F. Position a rack in the center. Lightly grease all 12 wells of a standard muffin tin with the neutral oil, using a pastry brush or paper towel to coat the bottoms and sides thoroughly.
  2. 02In a large bowl, break up the cooled cooked rice with your hands or a fork so there are no large clumps. The grains should be separated but still slightly sticky.
  3. 03Add the chopped, well-drained kimchi, 1/2 cup shredded cheddar, eggs, kimchi juice, mayonnaise, soy sauce, toasted sesame oil, sugar, salt, and black pepper to the rice.
  4. 04Sprinkle in the sliced scallions and 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds. Using a spatula or your hands, mix until everything is evenly combined and the rice is thoroughly coated in the egg mixture. The mixture should hold together when pressed; if it feels dry or crumbly, add 1-2 tsp more kimchi juice or water and mix again.
  5. 05Divide the mixture evenly among the 12 muffin wells (about 1/4 cup firmly packed mixture per well). Press the rice mixture down firmly into each well, making sure it fills the bottom and reaches up the sides slightly so it forms a cohesive molded cup shape as it bakes. Pack it tightly; this is what helps the cups hold together after baking.
  6. 06Top each portion with a generous pinch of the remaining 1/2 cup shredded cheddar, mounding it slightly in the center of each cup. The cheese will melt and fuse into the rice, helping to bind the tops and create crisp, browned edges.
  7. 07Bake for 18-22 minutes, until the cups are set, the edges are deeply golden and crisp, and the cheese on top is bubbling and browned in spots. Rotate the pan once halfway through for even browning.
  8. 08While the cups bake, prepare the gochujang drizzle. In a small bowl, whisk together the gochujang, honey, rice vinegar, water, and soy sauce until smooth and pourable. If it seems too thick, whisk in a few more drops of water until it reaches a thick-but-drizzling consistency. Set aside.
  9. 09Remove the muffin tin from the oven and place it on a wire rack. Let the cups cool in the pan for 8-10 minutes; this rest allows the starches and cheese to firm up so the rounds release cleanly and hold their molded shape.
  10. 10Run a thin knife or offset spatula around the edges of each cup to loosen. Gently lift each one out and transfer to a serving plate. You should see round, slightly flared rice cakes with crisp, cheesy sides that clearly mirror the muffin cup shape.
  11. 11Drizzle each rice cup lightly with the gochujang sauce, then sprinkle with extra scallions and toasted sesame seeds. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef's Notes

- Use properly cooled, slightly sticky short-grain rice; freshly cooked hot rice will steam in the cups instead of crisping and may fall apart. If your rice is very dry (day-old straight from the fridge), warm it just enough to soften, then cool again so it's pliable. - Kimchi saltiness varies; taste your mixture before adding the full 1/2 tsp salt and adjust. You want it well-seasoned but not harsh. - For extra crisp bottoms, preheat the greased muffin tin in the oven for 3-4 minutes before adding the rice mixture, then work quickly spooning and pressing it in. - These reheat well in a 375°F oven for 8-10 minutes. They're excellent alongside a simple cucumber salad or a fried egg if you want to turn them into a more substantial meal.
