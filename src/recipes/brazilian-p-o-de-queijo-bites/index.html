Brazilian Pão de Queijo Bites

Jump to Behind the Scenes

Why Muffin Pans?

These Brazilian pão de queijo bites bake up into round, chewy cheese puffs with crisp, golden edges that perfectly mirror the muffin cup's flared shape. Made with tapioca flour, sharp Parmesan, and nutty Minas-style cheese, they're naturally gluten-free and ideal for passing around at a party while still hot and stretchy inside.

Prep 20 mins
Cook 20 mins
Yield 24 servings
Difficulty Medium
Calories (approx) 80 per serving

Ingredients

  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 1/3 cup neutral oil (such as canola or grapeseed)
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 2 cups tapioca flour (also labeled tapioca starch)
  • 2 large eggs (room temperature)
  • 1 cup finely grated Parmesan cheese (Parmigiano Reggiano preferred, lightly packed)
  • 1 cup finely shredded queijo Minas padrão or low-moisture whole-milk mozzarella (lightly packed)
  • 2 tbsp finely grated Parmesan (for topping, optional)
  • 1 tbsp melted unsalted butter (for greasing, plus more if needed)

Instructions

  1. 01Adjust an oven rack to the center position and preheat the oven to 400°F. Place a 12-cup muffin tin on a sheet pan (for easier handling) and lightly brush each well with melted butter, coating the bottom and sides thoroughly.
  2. 02In a small saucepan, combine the milk, neutral oil, and salt. Heat over medium until the mixture just comes to a bare simmer with small bubbles at the edges, 3-5 minutes. Do not let it boil vigorously. Remove from heat.
  3. 03Place the tapioca flour in a medium mixing bowl. While whisking or stirring with a sturdy wooden spoon, slowly pour the hot milk mixture over the tapioca flour, stirring until it forms a thick, sticky dough and no dry pockets remain. It will look gummy and clumpy; that's correct. Let cool 5-7 minutes so it's warm but not hot to the touch.
  4. 04In a small bowl, lightly beat the eggs. Add the eggs to the warm tapioca mixture in 2 additions, stirring vigorously after each addition until fully incorporated. The dough will look separated at first, then smooth out into a glossy, stretchy batter.
  5. 05Add the 1 cup grated Parmesan and 1 cup shredded Minas cheese (or mozzarella) to the bowl. Stir until the cheeses are evenly distributed and the batter is thick but scoopable and slightly elastic. If it seems extremely runny, let it sit 5 minutes; tapioca will thicken as it hydrates.
  6. 06Using a tablespoon measure or small cookie scoop, portion about 2 level tbsp of batter into each muffin well. The batter should come to about halfway up the sides of each cup; you'll get 24 bites, so plan on baking in 2 batches or using a second pan if you have one. If desired, sprinkle a pinch of the extra 2 tbsp Parmesan over the top of each portion.
  7. 07Bake at 400°F for 18-20 minutes, until the bites are puffed, rounded, and deeply golden on the ridged sides and lightly browned on top. They should pull slightly away from the edges of the wells and feel set but still a bit springy when gently pressed.
  8. 08Remove the pan from the oven and let the bites rest in the muffin tin for 3-4 minutes to firm up and finish steaming inside. Run a thin knife or offset spatula around the edge of each well, then gently lift out each pão de queijo bite. The sides should show the distinct flared, molded shape from the muffin cups.
  9. 09Repeat filling and baking with the remaining batter, greasing the muffin wells lightly again if needed. Serve the bites warm on a platter, or keep them loosely tented with foil at room temperature for up to 1 hour.

Chef's Notes

- Tapioca flour is non-negotiable here; it's what gives pão de queijo their signature chewy, stretchy interior. Bob's Red Mill tapioca flour works well. - The dough is meant to be sticky and elastic. If it's too stiff to scoop, whisk in 1-2 tbsp milk; if it's soupy, let it sit a few minutes before scooping. - Queijo Minas padrão is ideal; if you can't find it, a mix of Parmesan and whole-milk mozzarella gives a good balance of flavor and melt. - For a party, you can bake them up to 2 hours ahead and rewarm in the muffin tin at 325°F for 5-7 minutes to re-crisp the sides while preserving that molded, rounded shape. Serve with chimichurri, spicy Brazilian-style malagueta oil, or just good salted butter.
