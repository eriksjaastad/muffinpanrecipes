Cardamom Pistachio Spiral Cups

Jump to Behind the Scenes

Why Muffin Pans?

These spiral cups wrap classic cinnamon-roll comfort around warm cardamom, orange, and pistachio for a Middle Eastern-inspired twist. A rich, buttery dough is coiled into each muffin cup so it bakes into a round, self-contained roll with crisp edges, tender centers, and a glossy orange-rose glaze.

Prep 40 mins
Cook 18 mins
Yield 12 servings
Difficulty Medium

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup Whole milk (warm, about 110°F; for the dough)
  • 2 tsp Active dry yeast (for the dough)
  • 1/4 cup + 2 tbsp Granulated sugar (1/4 cup for the dough, 2 tbsp for dusting muffin cups)
  • 2 1/4 cups + 2-4 tbsp All-purpose flour (2 1/4 cups for the dough, plus 2-4 tbsp extra as needed for kneading)
  • 1/2 tsp + 1/4 tsp + pinch Fine sea salt (1/2 tsp for the dough, 1/4 tsp for the filling, pinch for the glaze)
  • 1 1/2 tsp Ground cardamom (1 tsp for the dough, 1/2 tsp for the filling)
  • 1 Large egg (room temperature; for the dough)
  • 9 tbsp Unsalted butter (4 tbsp softened for the dough, 4 tbsp very soft for the filling, 1 tbsp melted for the glaze)
  • 1/2 cup Light brown sugar (packed; for the filling)
  • 1 1/2 tbsp Ground cinnamon (for the filling)
  • 1/3 cup + 2 tbsp Pistachios (finely chopped, unsalted, shelled; 1/3 cup for the filling, 2 tbsp for garnish)
  • 2 tbsp Unsalted butter (melted; for greasing muffin pan)
  • 1 cup Powdered sugar (sifted; for the glaze)
  • 2 tbsp + 1-2 tsp Fresh orange juice (2 tbsp for the glaze, plus more as needed to thin)
  • 1 tsp + extra Finely grated orange zest (1 tsp for the glaze, extra for garnish)
  • 1/2 tsp Rose water (or to taste; for the glaze)

Instructions

  1. 01Prepare the dough: Warm the milk to about 110°F - warm to the touch, not hot. In a small bowl, stir together the warm milk, yeast, and 1 tbsp of the granulated sugar from the 1/4 cup. Let sit 5-10 minutes until foamy on top.
  2. 02In a large mixing bowl, whisk together 2 1/4 cups flour, the remaining granulated sugar from the 1/4 cup, 1/2 tsp fine sea salt, and 1 tsp ground cardamom.
  3. 03Add the foamy yeast mixture and the egg to the dry ingredients. Stir with a wooden spoon until a shaggy dough forms.
  4. 04Add 4 tbsp softened unsalted butter and work it into the dough. Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface and knead 7-9 minutes, adding 1-3 tbsp of the extra flour only as needed, until the dough is smooth, elastic, and just slightly tacky but not sticky.
  5. 05Shape into a ball, place in a lightly greased bowl, cover tightly, and let rise in a warm spot until doubled, about 1 hour.
  6. 06Prepare the muffin pan: While the dough rises, thoroughly brush all 12 wells of a standard muffin tin with 2 tbsp melted unsalted butter, making sure to coat the sides and bottom of each cup.
  7. 07Sprinkle about 1/2 tsp of the 2 tbsp granulated sugar into each greased well, rotating and tapping so the sugar lightly coats the bottom and lower sides. This sugared butter layer helps the spirals caramelize and set into a firm, molded shape that releases cleanly.
  8. 08Make the filling: In a small bowl, mix 4 tbsp very soft unsalted butter, brown sugar, cinnamon, 1/2 tsp ground cardamom, and 1/4 tsp fine sea salt until it forms a smooth, spreadable paste.
  9. 09Stir in 1/3 cup finely chopped pistachios. Set aside at room temperature (you want it soft, not chilled).
  10. 10Shape the spiral cups: Punch down the risen dough gently to deflate. Turn it out onto a lightly floured surface and roll into a rectangle about 12 inches by 9 inches, with the long side facing you. Try to keep the thickness even, about 1/4 inch.
  11. 11Spread the filling evenly over the dough, leaving a 1/2-inch border along the long top edge bare.
  12. 12Starting from the long edge closest to you, roll the dough up tightly into a log, pinching the seam along the bare edge to seal well.
  13. 13Trim off the uneven ends if needed. Using a sharp knife or unflavored dental floss, cut the log into 12 equal slices, each about 1 inch thick.
  14. 14Take each slice and, with your fingers, gently tuck any loose tail of dough underneath so you have a compact, round spiral. Place each slice cut-side up into a prepared muffin cup, centering it so it touches the bottom and has a little space around the sides. As they rise and bake, they'll expand to fill the round, flared shape of the wells.
  15. 15Let the spirals rise in the pan: Cover the filled muffin tin loosely with lightly greased plastic wrap or a clean towel.
  16. 16Let rise in a warm place until the spirals are puffed and nearly fill the cups, about 25-35 minutes. During this rise they'll settle into the round muffin shape that will hold once baked.
  17. 17Bake: Preheat the oven to 350°F.
  18. 18When the spirals are puffed and the dough springs back slowly when gently pressed, place the muffin tin on the center rack.
  19. 19Bake 16-18 minutes, rotating the pan halfway through, until the spiral cups are golden brown on top and the edges where dough meets pan are a deeper golden caramel. The sugar at the bottom will bubble and set, helping the cups hold together.
  20. 20Remove from the oven and set the pan on a wire rack. Let the cups cool in the pan for exactly 5-7 minutes - this allows the caramelized sugar base to firm so each portion unmolds as a cohesive, molded round.
  21. 21Release from the pan: After the brief rest, run a thin offset spatula or butter knife around the inside edge of each cup to loosen the sides, keeping the blade tight against the metal so you don't tear the roll.
  22. 22Place a baking sheet or large plate over the muffin tin and, holding both together with oven mitts, invert quickly. Lift the muffin tin off; the spiral cups should drop out, each with a lightly caramelized, flat base and rounded, molded sides.
  23. 23If any resist, lift the pan back off, loosen that cup a bit more with the knife, and invert again. Turn each spiral cup right-side up so the spiral faces up on the cooling rack or serving platter.
  24. 24Make the orange-rose glaze: In a small bowl, whisk together powdered sugar, 2 tbsp orange juice, 1 tsp orange zest, rose water, 1 tbsp melted unsalted butter, and a pinch of fine sea salt until smooth and pourable.
  25. 25If too thick, whisk in 1/2 tsp more orange juice at a time (from the remaining 1-2 tsp) until it ribbons off the whisk and slowly disappears back into the bowl. If too thin, add 1-2 tbsp more powdered sugar.
  26. 26Glaze and garnish: While the spiral cups are still slightly warm but not hot (about 10-15 minutes after unmolding), spoon or drizzle the glaze over the tops, letting it run into the spirals and down the molded sides.
  27. 27Sprinkle with 2 tbsp finely chopped pistachios and a touch of extra orange zest, if using. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef's Notes

The sugared, buttered muffin cups are non-negotiable: they create a thin caramelized shell at the base and sides, which is what lets each cup hold its round, molded shape after you lift it from the pan. The dough should be soft and slightly tacky; adding too much flour makes tight, dry spirals that won't expand to fill the wells. If it's sticking badly to your hands after a few minutes of kneading, then add flour 1 tbsp at a time. Rose water varies in strength; start with 1/2 tsp, taste the glaze, and only add a few extra drops if you want more floral aroma. You're aiming for fragrance, not perfume. These are best the day they're baked, but they reheat well: 5-7 minutes in a 300°F oven will refresh the crumb and re-set the glaze just enough without melting it off.
