About Muffin Pan Recipes

A workplace sitcom set in a small muffin bakery. The staff are AI characters, and each week's recipe is what their week produces.

Muffin Pan Recipes is a workplace sitcom whose cast are AI characters running a small-town bakery that makes muffin-pan recipes. None of them are real people. A new recipe publishes every Sunday.

Sources: docs/CREATIVE_BIBLE.md, commit dd97a4d.

January 2026: a recipe site first

The repository began on January 3 as a recipe engine and editorial site, which launched January 4. There were no characters yet.

Sources: commits 1b1d080 (2026-01-03) and 10beea9 (2026-01-04).

Late January: one agent per character

A January 14 brainstorm proposed a writers' room working "like sitcom writers." The January 22 build gave each character its own agent, with a history, quirks, relationships, and a memory of past tasks.

Sources: commit 0a5d74a (2026-01-22); backend/data/agent_personalities.json.

February 2026: the bakery sitcom

The Creative Bible made the site the bakery sitcom it is today, the origin of its current identity. Each recipe is a six-day story arc told in the team's group chat, with dialogue rules borrowed from Friends, Seinfeld, and Cheers. Its test: strip the names off a conversation and see whether you can still tell who is talking.

Sources: docs/CREATIVE_BIBLE.md, commits dd97a4d and 5eb63f6 (2026-02-25).

What changed

That test was the hard part. A September read of 917 published lines found the characters in nearly the same register, each line a separate model call carrying the speaker's backstory, voice guide, and relationships. In October, production switched to one author writing each day's full scene. On one week, the line-by-line generator passed the judge on the first try on 1 of 6 days; the author passed 6 of 6. That is one week and a model judge, not proof, and a blind human comparison of Monday meetings split 7 to 7.

Sources: docs/conversation-lab/DIALS.md; commit bf44248; docs/conversation-lab/RESEARCH_PLAN.md.

To be direct: these recipes are generated by AI, not tested in a physical kitchen, unless a recipe page explicitly says otherwise. Read one the way you'd read one from a stranger's blog, and check for doneness rather than trusting the clock alone.

The site itself, including the code, the pipeline, and the decision to run it this way, is built and maintained by a human, Erik Sjaastad.
